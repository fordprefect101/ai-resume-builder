What matters in tech work, so you know which facts to look for:

- The thing: what was built, changed, fixed or run, and who or what it was for.
- The person's own part: what they did themselves, as opposed to the team.
- Tools: languages, frameworks, databases, cloud services, build and deploy tools. Capture each one named, using the person's own wording (for example "k8s" stays "k8s" in the quote).
- Scale: users, requests, orders, rows, services, team size, how often something runs.
- Speed and reliability: latency, load time, uptime, failures, how often something broke.
- Results: what changed afterwards, including plain statements with no numbers ("the analysts stopped pinging us every morning").
- Money and time saved, only when the person says it.

Good facts are small and checkable:
- "Rewrote the parsing step of the data pipeline" (action, me)
- "The pipeline is written in Python and scheduled with Airflow" (tool)
- "Analysts stopped asking about broken data every morning" (result)

Not facts: opinions about quality ("it was great"), things the story only implies, and anything about the person's feelings or the company's strategy.
